Wild Color Roulette in UNO No Mercy: Rules and What You Draw

Wild Color Roulette is a wild card in UNO No Mercy. You play it on any card. The next player names a color, then draws from the deck until a card of that color turns up. They keep everything they drew, the match included, and lose their turn. Nothing can be stacked on it.

What the card does and how many are in the deck

Color Roulette is one of four wild types in the No Mercy deck. There are eight copies among the 168 cards, the same count as the Wild Reverse Draw 4. The Draw 6 and the Draw 10 have four each, for 24 wilds in all.

It is the only penalty card with no number on it. A Draw 10 costs ten cards. Color Roulette costs however many it takes for one color to appear: one card if the victim is lucky, a dozen if not.

When you can play it

It is a wild, so on a normal turn it goes on any top card in any color. No match is needed.

The one condition is that no draw stack can be live. While a draw penalty is waiting to be taken, only draw cards that can stack are legal. Color Roulette has a draw value of zero, so the game will not let you play it there. Keep it for a clean turn.

You do not pick a color when you play it. That choice belongs to the next player.

What happens next

1. The turn passes. Play moves to the next player in the current direction, skipping anyone already eliminated.

2. The victim names a color. In Open Mercy a ROULETTE TRAP DETECTED window opens with four buttons, each showing how many cards of that color they already hold.

3. They draw one card at a time. Every card goes into their hand, and the 25-card limit is checked after each one.

4. The draw stops on the first card of the named color. Wilds do not count. A Draw 10 or another Color Roulette is one more card in the hand.

5. The match is kept. It is not played or discarded. It stays in the hand with everything drawn before it, so even a first-card match costs one card.

6. The victim's turn is over. The Color Roulette stays on top of the discard pile, the active color becomes the one the victim named, and play moves to the player after them.

A worked example

Play is going Priya, Marcus, Jo, Sam. Marcus plays Color Roulette. Jo names blue. She draws a red 7, then a Draw 10, which is wild and does not count, then a yellow Skip, then a blue 4. She keeps all four, her turn ends, and Sam plays next on blue.

Edge cases and the mistake people make

The deck runs out mid-draw

The discard pile, minus the Color Roulette on top, is shuffled into a fresh draw pile and the draw carries on. If there are still no cards to draw, the draw stops. The victim keeps what they drew, their turn ends, and the active color stays what it was before the Roulette.

The draw reaches 25 cards

The mercy rule is checked after every card. If a Roulette draw takes a hand to 25, that player is out at that moment, even if the card that did it was the match. If only one player is left, the game ends there.

Color Roulette as your last card

You win the moment it lands. The next player never draws.

The thrower does not pick the color. In Open Mercy the choice belongs to the player who has to draw. The other mistake is trying to answer it with a draw card. There is no stack to join. The victim names a color and draws.

Strategy

  • Aim it at the biggest hand. Every draw is a mercy-rule check. A player on 18 cards can be eliminated by a single Roulette, which no fixed draw card can promise.
  • As the victim, name the color you have seen least. Each color has 37 cards. If few reds have been played and you hold few, more are still in the deck and the draw tends to end sooner.
  • Do not hold it as a defense. It cannot join a stack, so it will not save you from a +6. Play it on a clean turn.
  • With two players it is a draw plus a skip. Your opponent draws and the turn comes straight back to you, on the color they chose.
  • Check the direction first. A Reverse earlier in the round may have put a different player in the firing line.

Questions people ask

How many cards do you draw on Color Roulette?

There is no fixed number. You draw one card at a time until a card of the color you named appears, and that card is the last draw. Wilds do not end it. It can be one card or it can take you to 25 and out.

Can you stack on Color Roulette?

No. Color Roulette has no draw value, so it cannot be played onto a live draw stack, and no Draw 2, Draw 4, Draw 6 or Draw 10 can be played in reply to it. The next player must name a color and draw.

Who picks the color on Color Roulette?

The next player, the one who has to draw. The player who throws the card gets no say. In Open Mercy four color buttons appear on the victim's screen, and play continues in the color they pick.

How does Color Roulette work with 2 players?

The same way, with the turn coming straight back. Your opponent names a color, draws until it appears, keeps the cards, and loses their turn. It is then your turn again, on the color they named. In a two-player game it is a draw card and a Skip in one.

Do you keep the card that matches the color?

Yes. The matching card stays in your hand with everything drawn before it. It is not played or discarded, so even a first-card match costs one card. The color you named becomes the active color for the next player.

What happens if the deck runs out during Color Roulette?

The discard pile, except the Color Roulette on top, is shuffled into a new draw pile and the draw continues. If there are still no cards, the draw stops. You keep what you drew, your turn ends, and the active color stays as it was.
