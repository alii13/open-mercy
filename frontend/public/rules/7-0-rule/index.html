The 7 and 0 Rule in UNO No Mercy: Swap and Rotate Hands

In UNO No Mercy, a 7 lets you swap your whole hand with any other player still in the game, and a 0 makes every player pass their hand one seat in the direction of play. Both are part of the No Mercy rules, not house rules. The rotation is automatic; you choose the swap target.

What the 7 does

Play a 7 and you may trade your entire hand for another player's. You pick the player. Anyone still in the game is a legal target, including a player down to a single card; eliminated players can't be chosen. The 7 itself is already on the discard pile, so it is not part of the trade. Afterwards the turn passes to the next player as normal.

In Open Mercy a SWAP HANDS window lists every opponent with their card count. Pick one, or press KEEP MY HAND and the 7 plays like a plain number. The printed rule has you swap; the button exists because being forced to trade a 4-card hand for a 9-card one helps nobody.

What the 0 does

Play a 0 and every player still in the game passes their whole hand to the next seat in the current direction of play. Nobody chooses anything. It happens the moment the 0 lands, and then the turn moves on.

Your hand goes to the player after you. You receive the hand of the player who plays before you. That second part is what people miss.

Example. Play is going Priya, Marcus, Jo, Sam. Priya plays a 0. Priya's cards go to Marcus, Marcus's to Jo, Jo's to Sam, and Sam's come round to Priya. After a Reverse the hands travel the other way: Priya's would go to Sam and she would receive Marcus's. Eliminated seats are skipped.

Is the 7-0 rule official in No Mercy?

Yes. In classic UNO, 7-0 is a house rule the table agrees to before dealing. In UNO No Mercy it is part of the ruleset, alongside stacking, the bigger draw cards and the 25-card mercy limit. Nobody asks whether the table is playing 7-0. Open Mercy has no switch to turn it off: every 7 offers a swap and every 0 rotates.

When you can play a 7 or a 0

They follow the number-card rules: a 7 goes on its color or on another 7, a 0 on its color or on another 0. After a wild, the chosen color counts.

  • Not onto a live draw stack. Only draw cards are legal while a stack is waiting. See the stacking rules.
  • As the opening card. A 7 or 0 turned over first does nothing. It only sets the starting color.
  • On top of a Discard All. The card you choose for the top fires its effect: a 7 gives you the swap prompt, a 0 rotates the hands.
  • As your last card. A 7 played as your final card wins on the spot. No swap happens.

Edge cases and the mistake people make

One-card hands

You can swap with a player who holds one card. That is usually the point. Two things follow from how the game checks the last-card call.

  • A hand you were given needs no call. The call is due when your own play takes you from two cards to one. A swap or rotation that leaves you on one card expects no call, nobody can catch you, and playing that card wins.
  • If the 7 itself takes you from two cards to one, press MERCY! before you throw it, then choose who to swap with.

Near the 25-card limit

A swap or rotation never eliminates anyone by itself; only drawing does. Receive 24 cards from a 7 and you are still in, but your next draw ends it.

The mistake with the 7 is swapping on reflex: hold 5, swap into 4, and you gained one card and told everyone you had a 7. The mistake with the 0 is forgetting you receive a hand too: shed 15 while the player before you holds 20, and you traded up.

Strategy

  • Hold a 7 while your hand is big. Spend it on the smallest hand at the table, ideally a player on one or two cards.
  • Count before you swap. If nobody has fewer cards than you, press KEEP MY HAND.
  • Before a 0, look at who plays before you: that is the hand you get. If it is bigger than yours, hold the 0 or play a Reverse first.
  • Finish a Discard All with a 7 or a 0 on top when you can. The effect comes free with the dump.
  • With two players a 0 is a swap you cannot decline. Only play it when your opponent holds fewer cards.

Common questions

Is the 7-0 rule official in UNO No Mercy?

Yes. The 7 swap and the 0 rotation are part of the UNO No Mercy rules, not an optional add-on. In classic UNO the same idea exists only as a house rule the table agrees to in advance. In No Mercy, every 7 and every 0 does this in every game.

Do you have to swap hands when you play a 7?

Under the printed rule, yes. Open Mercy adds a KEEP MY HAND button so you can play a 7 as a plain number when nobody has fewer cards than you. Either way, you choose who you swap with, and that player gets no say.

Which way do hands rotate on a 0?

In the direction of play. Your hand goes to the player who takes the next turn, and you receive the hand of the player who went before you. After a Reverse the hands travel the other way. Eliminated players are skipped; every hand still in the game moves one seat.

How does the 7-0 rule work with 2 players?

A 7 has one possible target, so you trade hands with your opponent, or keep your hand in Open Mercy. A 0 passes each hand to the only other seat, so it is a swap too, with no option to decline. Direction does not matter with two players.

Can you swap with a player who has one card?

Yes, and it is the strongest use of a 7. Any player still in the game is a legal target, however few cards they hold. You take their single card and they take your whole hand. You did not play down to one card yourself, so no MERCY! call is needed, and playing that card wins.
